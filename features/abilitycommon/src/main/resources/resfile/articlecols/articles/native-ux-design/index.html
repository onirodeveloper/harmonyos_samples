1.2 Why multi-device UX design is needed

Common experience pain points on large screens:

  • Large images and text, little information, truncated or distorted content, no landscape support, and excessive whitespace.
  • You want to design well for large screens but do not know how to add value to the experience.

1.3 How to create multi-device UX design

Understand the physical differences between devices and their human-factor impact. Consider the best interaction methods, information volume, and content layout, then explore value-added experiences for large screens based on the principles of “more content,” “greater immersion,” “higher efficiency,” and “hover innovation.”

1.6 Value-added experience

Value-added experience design methods

Show more

Read and review at the same time: on large-screen devices, provide comments, related recommendations, keyword analysis, and other helpful information on the right when reading text and images.

Greater immersion

Immersive viewing: play videos full screen on foldable devices without rotating the screen or interrupting the viewing experience.

Pinned danmaku: display danmaku in black areas whenever possible to reduce obstruction of the video.

Side panel: perform related operations in a side panel while playing a video. Push the video aside to avoid covering it.

Higher efficiency

  • Efficient columns: use columns to improve efficiency on selected pages of finance, e-commerce, lifestyle, and news applications.

  • In-app split screen: enable document collaboration in office scenarios; in IM conversations, provide in-app split screen when opening web pages, atomic services, documents, or calls for a chat-and-view experience; use it for price comparison while shopping.
